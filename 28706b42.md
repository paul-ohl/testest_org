# The survivor bias in the case of inclusivity

The following argument is often said when it comes to changing urbanism:

> "Why make (sometimes expensive) infrastructure for such a small percentage of
> the population?"

There are two problems with this argument:

## Probably not a small percentage

The main reason why people with disabilities or cyclists are almost absent from
some urban spaces is because these urban spaces are *hostile* to them. If a
space is deadly it will naturally be avoided.

But it also means that these spaces will be visited again once they are made
inclusive.

## The infrastructure is not only for that minority

There are countless examples of that, (good) bike infrastructure benefits cars,
inclusive infrastructure benefits people with disabilities, but also people with
strollers or suitcases.

Inclusive infrastructure is almost never at a loss, because it encourages more
people to take part in the life of a city. And even if it were, a strong
argument can be made that it should be done anyway.

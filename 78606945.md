# Candle wax

The candle wax is the central element of the candle.

The goal is to be able to control the shape, color, smell and melting point of
the wax in order to obtain the most pleasing result possible.

The main wax types used for DIY candles are **soy**, **paraffin**, **coconut**
and obviously **beeswax**.

## Wax melting temperatures chart

| Candle Wax Type | Melting Range | Notable Characteristics                                                  |
| --------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Coconut Wax     | 49–54°C       | Creamy texture, often blended with other waxes for better stability.     |
| Soy Wax         | 49–60°C       | Natural, good for container candles, often used in eco-friendly lines.   |
| Paraffin Wax    | 49–71°C       | Affordable, versatile, and widely used in commercial candles.            |
| Palm Wax        | 54–60°C       | Crystallizes upon cooling, creating unique surface patterns.             |
| Beeswax         | 62–65°C       | Naturally higher melting point, pleasant honey aroma, slow burning.      |
| Microcrystalline| 65–82°C       | Derived from petroleum, adds hardness and raises melting point in blends.|
| Polyethylene    | 85–110°C      | Highly effective for increasing overall melt temperature and hardness.   |
